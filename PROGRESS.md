8. Supabase free-tier Realtime limits (read from the docs on 2026-11-04): 100 messages/s, 100 channel joins/s, 100 channels per connection, 256 KB broadcast payload, 20 presence messages/s, 2 million messages/month, 200 peak connections. A broadcast counts as 1 sent plus 1 per subscribed receiver. The docs do not say whether the 100/s cap counts deliveries, so assume it does. Sources: supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/limits, /pricing, and /guides/platform/manage-your-usage/realtime-messages.
9. Message-budget consequence for the N2 [design] note (my estimate): 4 humans each sending at 15 Hz is 60 sends/s x (1 + 3 receivers) = about 240 messages/s per room, 2.4x the 100/s cap; at 10 Hz about 160/s. 2 humans + bots (all bots in one message from the host) at 15 Hz is about 90/s. At 240/s the monthly 2M would last about 8,300 s of racing (roughly 90 races of 90 s). HANDOFF §6.3 assumed about 180/s and did not count the sender. Options for the design note: lower send rate plus interpolation, send only on change, drop to 2-3 real senders per room, or a paid plan. This needs the user's decision before N2.
10. Supabase now issues publishable keys (sb_publishable_...) instead of anon keys. The publishable key is stored in VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY (name kept so the harness, CI and deploy secrets keep working). The secret key (sb_secret_...) and service_role are never used; the secrets smoke check fails if one appears.

36. How an obstacle is written in a course (src/logic/obstacles.js, validated by validateCourse): `col`/`row` are the CENTRE in tiles, fractions allowed (the pivot for a bar and a hammer), not the top-left corner that ground blocks use; angles are clockwise-positive in radians (degrees only in `ampDeg`); a bar is `length`, `rps` (signed), optional `thick`, `phase`; a hammer is `length` (arm), `ampDeg`, `period`, optional `head`, `phase`; a pusher wall and a platform are `w`, `h`, `dx`, `dy` (the trip), `period`, optional `phase`, and move with a sine ease so each trip starts and ends at rest. Only the hammer's head is solid; its arm is a drawn chain you can run through. The race clock holds every obstacle at its t = 0 pose while t is below 0 (the countdown).
37. What counts as a hit (src/logic/obstacles.js `hitFrom`, decided on `collisionstart` and `collisionactive`): only a bar or a hammer, only if the touched point moves faster than `hitMinSpeed`, and only if the runner is not still immune. The direction is the way that point is moving; if it is moving mostly up or down, away from the obstacle's middle. The strength is the same default hit as G1's, not scaled by swing speed, so every hit is the 4–6 tile tumble from the feel target (measured by the e2e in both browsers: 4.4–4.8 tiles; the bar's number varies with where the bar is when it touches). The speed is worked out from the pose function, not read from Matter, and the runner's centre stands in for the exact contact point: close enough for a bar that is 3.6 tiles long. A pusher wall pushes and a moving platform carries, which Matter and the ground frame do (the e2e shows a runner pushed 1.00 tile, and carried 1.5 tiles: the test waits until the platform has moved 1.5 tiles, then checks the runner went with it). The HANDOFF §5.2 sentence "the `true` updates velocity, so a moving hammer actually knocks runners back" is NOT what happened: see Decision 40.
